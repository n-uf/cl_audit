My pick: B · Edge. It marks every surface without touching the usage or mint colours.

Notes

Rules every option keeps

  • Red, green and amber stay usage levels. None of the ten colours is one of them; bars, percentages and the full display keep their level colours.
  • Colour is a mark, never text. Indigo (3.2:1), purple (3.4:1) and blue (3.9:1) would fail as small text on the dark card.
  • Colour sits on the name line; state sits on the address line. The green and mint lamps stay under the name, so a teal or cyan mark never reads as a lamp.
  • Silver face: the colour is darkened to 60% with a dark hairline, so all ten reach 3.3:1 or more on #d0d2d3 (raw, yellow and lime are 1.3:1).
  • One clash in B and C: an orange profile selected on the tile bar has an orange slot on top and an orange edge below. If that is too much, move the slot, not the profile colour.

How each option behaves

A · DotB · EdgeC · Edge + wash
"By usage" A · A graphite dot with a pale ring, in the same place, so rows line up. B · No edge: the surface looks as it does today. C · A graphite edge and wash, close to today's grey; the Dock icon stays plain.
Legibility A · 3.2:1 or more on dark; darkened and ringed on Silver. Teal and cyan, blue and indigo are hard to tell apart at 7 px. B · No text sits on the line, so only line against face matters: 3.2:1 or more on dark, 3.3:1 on Silver. C · A 14 to 30% tint keeps the legend readable on all ten (rows below).
Usage levels A · A dot can't be mistaken for a bar. B · Edges sit outside; level colours stay inside as bars and numbers. C · The wash is the risk (orange or yellow behind amber, lime behind green), so it stops above the bars.
Selected, in use A · Pressed key, orange slot, mint display and green lamp are untouched. Weakest at a glance. B · Slot on top, colour at the foot; the mint display and lamp are away from the edge. C · The selected tile is tinted more (42% against 30%). Teal and cyan sit close to the mint display.
