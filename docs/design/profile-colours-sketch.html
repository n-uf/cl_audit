Profile colours: where they show

cl_audit design proposal · 2026-11-21 · nothing is built

Each profile already has a colour, picked in Settings ▸ Profile ▸ Colour from ten swatches. Today it shows in only a few places: the Dock icon (in the icon styles that use it), the dot at the head of the menu bar menu, and the usage-history chart. It does not show on the dashboard's tile bar, on the profile card's header, or in quick chat. These are three ways to carry it, from a dot to a tint. Samples only: profiles Main, Work and Studio, with example.com addresses.

By usagegraphite #3f3f46

Each chip: the colour, then its contrast against the Std dark card (#2a2a2d) and, after the Silver darkening described below, against the Silver plate (#d0d2d3). Raw on Silver, none of the ten reaches 3:1 (yellow and lime sit at 1.3).

  • Red, green and amber stay usage levels. The ten colours are chosen so none is plain red, green or amber; the usage bars, percentages and the full countdown keep their level colours in every option.
  • Colour is a mark, never the text. Names, addresses and numbers keep the face's own text colour. Indigo (3.2:1), purple (3.4:1) and blue (3.9:1) would fail as small text on the dark card, so no option colours text.
  • The colour sits on the name line; state sits on the address line. The mint and green lamps (in use, signed in) stay under the name, so a teal or cyan profile mark never reads as a lamp.
  • On the Silver face the colour is drawn darkened to 60% (every one then reaches 3.3:1 or more) with a dark hairline; on dark faces it is drawn as picked.

  • Usage levels The edges run along the foot or side of a surface; the level colours live inside it as bars and numbers, so an amber bar never touches a yellow edge. Colours close to a level (lime beside green, orange beside amber) are still separated by position.

  • Legibility Tint at 14 to 30% behind text keeps the legend readable on all ten on the dark face. On Silver the tint is light and the dark legend wins. The ten-colour row above shows it; yellow and lime make the brightest tints, still readable.
  • Usage levels The wash is the risk: an orange or yellow wash behind an amber bar, or a lime one behind a green bar, blurs "this is the profile" with "this is the level". That is why the wash stops at the header and never covers the bars.
  • Selected, in use The selected tile's wash is stronger (42% against 30%), so selected and colour stack on one key; the pressed look, orange slot and mint display still show. Teal and cyan washes sit close to the mint display next to them.

My pick

B · Edge. It makes the colour visible on every surface listed, at the size where a profile can be told from its neighbours at a glance, and it does it without touching what the other colours already mean.

  • The usage colours (red, amber, green) and the mint in-use treatment live inside a surface, as bars, numbers, lamps and displays. An edge sits on the outside, so the two never overlap, which a wash (C) cannot promise for yellow, orange or lime profiles.
  • A dot (A) is the safest, but at 7 px it does not carry colour across a dashboard of three or more profiles, and it reads like the status lamps beside it.
  • It is cheap and consistent: one rule, "a thin line in the profile colour", and a profile on "By usage" simply has none.
  • The one known clash is an orange profile selected on the tile bar: orange slot above, orange edge below. If that looks like too much in the app, the slot is the part to move to the face's mint, not the profile colour.

Not drawn: the menu, Dock and chat on macOS's light appearance; the Silver face for them is the same marks, darkened as above. To check in the app: all ten colours on both faces, and the Dock icon edge in each of the icon styles.
